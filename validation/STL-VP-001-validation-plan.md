## 1. Purpose

This Validation Plan defines the scope, lifecycle approach, responsibilities, deliverables, risk-management method, verification strategy, traceability expectations, deviation handling, acceptance criteria, and final decision process for the SampleTrack Lite mock computerized-system validation package.

The plan governs the exercise. It does not itself establish that SampleTrack Lite is validated or suitable for GxP production use.

## 2. Validation objective

The objective is to determine, within the explicitly fictional scenario and test environment, whether the configured SampleTrack Lite functions defined by the approved mock User Requirements Specification can be shown through risk-based verification to perform as intended for:

- receiving and inventory record control;
- controlled storage-location and status management;
- temperature-excursion identification and disposition workflow;
- chain-of-custody traceability;
- role-based security and authority control;
- GMP-relevant audit trails;
- electronic approval/signature behavior; and
- regulated-record retrieval.

The intended validation conclusion is bounded to the exact mock scope and evidence generated by this package.

## 3. Governing system boundary

The governing system and regulatory boundaries are defined by:

- `STL-SD-001` — System Description and Intended Use;
- `STL-RA-001` — Regulatory Applicability Statement.

Those documents are upstream authority for this plan.

If a later requirement conflicts materially with the intended-use or regulatory-applicability boundary, the conflict must be reconciled explicitly rather than silently expanding the system.

## 4. Regulatory and guidance basis

### 4.1 Primary Canadian basis

The exercise is primarily grounded in:

- the Food and Drugs Act and Food and Drug Regulations, Part C, Division 2, as the underlying Canadian GMP framework;
- Health Canada GUI-0001, including the expectation that wholesalers validate computerized systems used for GMP activities;
- Health Canada GUI-0050, Annex 11 to the GMP Guide: Computerized Systems;
- Health Canada GUI-0069 for environmental control of drugs during storage and transportation.

### 4.2 GAMP 5 Second Edition

GAMP 5 Second Edition is used as industry guidance for:

- lifecycle planning;
- risk-based scaling;
- configured-product considerations;
- supplier leverage;
- user requirements;
- verification and traceability;
- validation reporting.

The main application component is treated as a fictional configured product consistent with a Category 4 planning scenario.

Software category is not used as a prescriptive validation recipe. Validation rigor is to be scaled according to the GxP impact, complexity, novelty, configuration, and identified failure risks.

### 4.3 21 CFR Part 11 overlay

Selected Part 11 controls are included as a conditional electronic-record/electronic-signature exercise overlay.

This plan does not assert that the fictional Canadian operation is legally subject to Part 11.

Where Part 11 controls are included, they are treated as additional requirements to be verified within the mock scenario.

## 6. Scope

### 6.1 In scope

The mock validation will address the application functions identified in STL-SD-001, including:

- user authentication;
- role-based authorization;
- receiving and inventory record creation;
- storage-condition and storage-location assignment;
- controlled material statuses and transitions;
- temperature-excursion workflow;
- chain-of-custody history;
- quality disposition;
- GMP-relevant audit trails;
- electronic signatures for defined quality actions;
- record retrieval and human-readable output.

### 6.2 Outside the primary validation claim

Unless a later document adds bounded evidence, the following remain outside the primary claim:

- qualification of real servers, networks, databases, cloud infrastructure, or workstations;
- supplier quality-system audit;
- source-code review of a commercial supplier product;
- validation/qualification of temperature-monitoring hardware;
- warehouse temperature mapping;
- pharmaceutical stability assessment;
- automated ERP/WMS/LIMS/transport-system interfaces;
- real disaster-recovery infrastructure;
- real long-term archive performance;
- organizational SOP or training effectiveness;
- production deployment;
- actual Canadian, U.S., or EU regulatory compliance.

The roles below describe how a real validation exercise would separate responsibilities. No fictional named approvers or signatures will be created.

| Role | Responsibility in a real implementation | Mock-package treatment |

| Process Owner / Business SME | Defines intended process, critical business use, and acceptance needs | Represented through the approved mock intended-use and URS content |
| System Owner | Accountable for system lifecycle, availability, configuration, and validated state | Conceptual role only |
| Quality / Validation | Approves validation strategy, assesses deviations, reviews evidence, and supports release decision | Functions are represented in the documents; no fake approval is executed |
| Supplier | Provides product, lifecycle documentation, testing evidence, and quality information | Fictional supplier assumption only; no supplier evidence is invented |

One person may perform several activities in this training exercise. That does not make those activities independent.

## 9. Supplier and infrastructure strategy

### 9.1 Supplier

A real configured-product implementation would assess the supplier and determine which supplier evidence could be leveraged.

This mock exercise does not invent supplier audit results, development records, or certificates.

Supplier capability is therefore an explicit assumption and limitation.

### 9.2 Infrastructure

Health Canada GUI-0050 distinguishes application validation from IT infrastructure qualification.

No real production infrastructure exists for SampleTrack Lite in this exercise.

Accordingly:

- infrastructure qualification is not claimed;
- local demonstration-environment identity may be recorded for reproducibility;
- a successful local run does not establish infrastructure qualification;
- backup, recovery, availability, and continuity controls are treated as real-world dependencies unless specifically exercised within a later bounded demonstration.

## 10. Quality risk-management approach

### 10.1 Risk assessment purpose

STL-RSK-001 will identify plausible functional failure modes and use risk to determine verification depth.

The risk assessment will consider:

- patient/product-quality consequence;
- data-integrity consequence;
- regulatory-record consequence;
- probability of failure within the configured process;
- detectability before the failure causes harm;
- existing preventive/detective controls;
- residual risk after controls.

### 10.2 Scoring model

The exercise will use:

- Severity: 1–5;
- Probability: 1–5;
- Detectability: 1–5;
- supporting RPN: Severity × Probability × Detectability.

The numerical value is a prioritization aid, not the validation decision.

Risk thresholds and rating definitions must be defined in STL-RSK-001 before the system's individual failure modes are scored.

A maximum-severity failure may not be treated as low risk solely because probability or detectability produces a low arithmetic score.

### 10.3 Risk drives verification depth

Higher-risk functions should receive stronger opportunities to fail, such as:

- boundary-value cases;
- negative cases;
- unauthorized-role attempts;
- invalid data;
- incorrect workflow sequencing;
- audit-trail verification;
- electronic-signature verification;
- direct linkage between resulting evidence and the affected requirement.

Lower-risk convenience or presentation functions may receive lighter verification.

Test count alone is not the measure of assurance.

## 11. Qualification and verification strategy

### 11.1 IQ posture

A real implementation would establish installation/infrastructure prerequisites and verify the installed/configured product identity before functional qualification.

This mock package does **not** execute a formal production IQ because:

- no actual commercial SampleTrack Lite supplier product exists;
- no production infrastructure exists;
- no supplier installation package exists.

The plan will instead document the expected IQ boundary and preserve exact identity of any demonstration surrogate or test environment used later.

That surrogate-environment identity is evidence for reproducibility, not production IQ.

### 11.2 OQ posture

STL-OQ-001 is the primary executed qualification deliverable.

Approximately 18 risk-based test cases are planned.

The OQ should include, where applicable:

- normal functional paths;
- configured parameter boundaries;
- required-data limits;
- error handling;
- prohibited transitions;
- unauthorized-role attempts;
- audit-trail checks;
- electronic-signature checks;
- retrieval/output checks;
- one end-to-end scenario linking receipt through quality disposition.

Expected results must be defined before decisive execution.

### 11.3 PQ posture

A real PQ would demonstrate that the system supports the intended business process under representative operational conditions with trained users, approved procedures, realistic data, and production-like infrastructure.

This mock package does not claim a formal PQ because those conditions do not exist.

A realistic end-to-end OQ scenario may exercise the business workflow, but it will not be relabelled as PQ.

## 12. User Requirements Specification strategy

STL-URS-001 should:

- express what the regulated user/process needs;
- avoid unnecessary implementation detail;
- use objective, testable language;
- assign stable requirement IDs;
- identify GxP rationale and source mapping;
- remain traceable through risk assessment, testing, deviations, and final status.

The URS is expected to contain approximately 30–35 requirements.

Risk scores are not to be invented in the URS. Risk linkage will be added through STL-RSK-001 and STL-RTM-001.

## 13. Traceability strategy

STL-RTM-001 will provide a traceable path at least across:

```text
URS requirement
  -> risk / failure mode
  -> test case
  -> execution result
  -> evidence reference
  -> deviation where applicable
  -> final status
```

A requirement is not considered covered merely because a nearby test appears related.

Where one test supports several requirements, the specific coverage relationship must remain understandable.

## 14. Test evidence

Execution evidence should be sufficient to establish the actual result without producing a screenshot catalogue.

Each executed test should record, as appropriate:

- test ID;
- requirement and risk links;
- preconditions;
- test data;
- execution step;
- expected result;
- actual result;
- pass/fail;
- tester;
- actual execution date;
- evidence reference;
- deviation reference.

Evidence may include:

- screenshots;
- exported records;
- audit-trail output;
- system-generated reports;
- logs;
- structured result files.

Evidence identity may later be strengthened with checksums.

A checksum proves identity/integrity of the evidence object, not correctness of the system behavior shown by that object.

## 15. Pre-execution freeze

Before decisive OQ execution, the following should be sufficiently stable:

- applicable URS revision;
- risk assessment revision;
- test cases;
- test data where material;
- expected results;
- acceptance criteria;
- relevant configured behavior;
- exact demonstration-system identity.

Material changes after execution begins must be treated as deviations or successor test revisions when they could affect interpretation.

The first material failed result should not be overwritten by a later successful retest.

## 16. Validation deviations

A validation deviation is required when a material discrepancy occurs between:

- approved/expected procedure and actual execution;
- expected and actual system behavior;
- required and available evidence;
- test apparatus/environment and its intended state.

Deviation handling should record:

1. what was observed;
2. affected test/requirement/risk;
3. classification;
4. immediate containment where needed;
5. impact on prior or related evidence;
6. cause where established;
7. correction;
8. required retest;
9. final disposition;
10. effect on the final validation decision.

Three seeded training deviations are planned and will be identified as such.

They must remain visible after resolution.

## 17. Failure classification

Where useful, failures should be distinguished as:

- system/configuration failure;
- protocol/specification discrepancy;
- evidence/execution deficiency;
- test-apparatus/environment failure;
- unresolved/unknown.

Classification requires evidence.

A system failure must not be relabelled as a protocol failure merely because changing the protocol would make the test pass.

## 18. Change and configuration management

For the mock package, controlled configuration includes at least:

- user roles;
- permitted material statuses;
- status-transition rules;
- storage-location values;
- storage-condition values;
- temperature-excursion thresholds;
- electronic-signature settings where applicable.

Once decisive OQ begins, material configuration changes require:

- a recorded reason;
- affected requirements/risk review;
- deviation or change record as appropriate;
- identification of tests requiring re-execution;
- updated configuration identity.

A later successful configuration is not evidence that the earlier failed configuration never existed.

## 19. Acceptance criteria

The mock validation may reach a positive bounded release decision only if all of the following are satisfied:

1. the final in-scope URS is uniquely identified;
2. every in-scope URS requirement is traced to appropriate verification or has an explicit justified disposition;
3. all planned OQ tests are executed or formally dispositioned;
4. high-risk functions identified by STL-RSK-001 have verification proportionate to their risk;
5. no unresolved deviation remains that invalidates a high-risk requirement or the overall validation conclusion;
6. failed tests that require correction are resolved through documented impact assessment and justified retest;
7. access control, material-status authority, temperature-excursion control, audit-trail behavior, electronic-signature behavior, and regulated-record retrieval meet their applicable acceptance criteria;
8. the tested configuration/system identity is known;
9. the RTM reflects the final executed evidence;
10. residual risks and untested production dependencies are explicitly stated in STL-VSR-001.

Formal company QA approval is not fabricated for this exercise.

## 20. Conditions that block positive release

A positive mock release decision is blocked by any of the following:

- an untested or failed high-risk requirement without justified disposition;
- an unresolved defect permitting unauthorized release/disposition;
- an unresolved defect allowing an excursion to bypass required hold/review;
- materially unreliable or non-reviewable GMP audit-trail behavior;
- materially broken electronic-signature attribution/linkage for a required signed action;
- inability to retrieve required GxP records;
- a validation deviation that makes decisive evidence unreliable;
- identity/configuration drift such that the tested object cannot be established;
- incomplete traceability that prevents reconstruction of critical requirements.

## 21. Validation Summary Report

STL-VSR-001 will summarize:

- exact package and demonstration-system identity;
- executed deliverables;
- OQ results;
- deviations and retests;
- traceability status;
- residual risks;
- unverified production dependencies;
- final bounded decision.

The report should not state simply that "SampleTrack Lite is validated."

The conclusion must identify the scope actually supported by the evidence.

## 22. Operational lifecycle / revalidation triggers

In a real implementation, the validated state would require ongoing control.

Revalidation or documented impact assessment would be expected after changes such as:

- supplier software upgrade;
- material configuration change;
- new or changed workflow/status transition;
- changed excursion thresholds;
- changed electronic-signature behavior;
- changed authentication/authorization model;
- new external interface;
- infrastructure change affecting validated behavior;
- data migration;
- material incident or recurring deviation;
- regulatory change affecting intended use;
- periodic review identifying validation-state concern.

This mock package will record these as lifecycle expectations, not claim they have been operationally exercised.

## 23. Assumptions and limitations

This plan assumes the intended-use and regulatory-applicability documents remain materially unchanged during this slice.

The exercise does not establish:

- supplier qualification;
- infrastructure qualification;
- long-term operational reliability;
- real production backup/restore;
- actual regulatory acceptance;
- independent QA approval;
- formal PQ under production conditions.

Any later public description must preserve those boundaries.
